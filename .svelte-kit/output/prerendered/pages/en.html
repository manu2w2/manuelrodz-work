How I Think and Build

Beyond specific tools or programming languages, my work follows a consistent decision-making framework across engineering and collaborative leadership.

Learning in order to share

My primary drive is understanding how systems work and experimenting with available resources. Yet learning feels incomplete until it is shared clearly to help others grow—whether mentoring incoming robotics teammates or creating free visual study sessions for peers.

Problem-solving workflow

  1. 1. Deep problem analysis: Before fabricating parts or writing code, I break down the real constraints of the challenge, rulebook, and operating environment.
  2. 2. Mapping viable alternatives: I identify multiple technical or operational paths instead of committing prematurely to the first available idea.
  3. 3. Selecting by feasibility and resonance: I evaluate which solution is achievable within real deadlines and resources, and aligns best with the team’s strengths.
  4. 4. Digital validation and iterative execution: I validate assemblies digitally before manufacturing and refine every iteration using empirical test evidence.

FEV Engineering Methodology

In mechanical design and competitive robotics, every subsystem is evaluated against three core criteria:

Reliability (Fiabilidad)
Consistent mechanical and software response under real match conditions.
Efficiency (Eficiencia)
Maximizing output from every component, weight budget, and cycle second.
Feasibility (Viabilidad)
Manufacturable, maintainable, and realistic within our timeline and tools.
